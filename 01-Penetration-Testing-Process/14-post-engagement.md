# 📄 Section 14: Post-Engagement

**Module:** 01 — Penetration Testing Process  
**Topic:** Report Review and Finalization

---

## 📚 Overview

The post-engagement phase focuses on finalizing the penetration test, communicating findings, delivering documentation, and closing out the engagement.

A penetration test report is an important deliverable because it communicates identified vulnerabilities, supporting evidence, potential business impact, and recommended remediation actions.

Before the final report is issued, the client may be given an opportunity to review a draft and provide comments.

---

## ❓ Questions & Answers

### Q1. What designation do we typically give a report when it is first delivered to a client for a chance to review and comment? *(One word)*

#### ✅ Short Answer

**Answer: `DRAFT`**

#### 📝 Detailed Explanation

A penetration testing report is generally marked **DRAFT** when it is initially delivered to the client for review and feedback.

The draft allows the client to examine the report and identify potential factual errors, misunderstandings, or missing context before the final version is issued.

The review process may include the following steps:

1. **Prepare the report:** Document findings, evidence, risk assessments, and remediation recommendations.
2. **Mark it as DRAFT:** Make clear that the document is undergoing review and is not yet the finalized deliverable.
3. **Send it to the client:** Provide the draft to the appropriate stakeholders.
4. **Collect feedback:** Allow the client to comment on factual accuracy, technical details, and relevant business context.
5. **Validate comments:** Review feedback and make corrections when supported by evidence.
6. **Finalize the report:** Incorporate appropriate revisions and deliver the agreed final version.

The exact review and approval process depends on the engagement's contractual arrangements and reporting procedures.

### 🔍 Why This Matters

Clearly distinguishing a draft report from a final report helps manage expectations and prevents preliminary findings from being mistaken for an approved final deliverable.

It also provides an opportunity to correct errors and improve the clarity of the report before formal delivery.

Client feedback should not automatically change a technical finding. Findings and conclusions must remain consistent with the available evidence.

---

## 🧠 Quick Revision

**Question:** What designation is typically given to a report delivered initially for client review and comments?

**Answer:** **DRAFT**

**Remember:** A draft report is provided for review before the final report is issued.

**Key takeaway:** Marking a report as DRAFT communicates that it is still subject to review and potential revision.
